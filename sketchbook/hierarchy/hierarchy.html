Howard Roark laughed,

He stood naked at the edge of the cliff.


The lake lay far below him.

A frozen explosion of granite burst in flight to the sky over motionless water.


The water seems immovable,

the stone– flowing.
The stone had the stillness
of one brief moment in a battle when

thrust

meets

thrust

and the currents are held in a pause more dynamic than motion.

The stone flowed,

wet with sunrays.







The Fountainhead

by Ayn Rand





Reflection:


This exercise was fun because it allowed me to disregard some of the formal design rules of hierarchy that I have been taught in previous design courses. Usually, hierarchy is used to guide the reader's eye towards what is most important and create an order for how information should be read. For this assignment, I was able to do the opposite and use hierarchy to change how the original passage is interpreted.

I wanted to make the passage feel scarier and more threatening than it was originally intended to be. Instead of focusing on Howard, I wanted the reader's attention to shift toward the stone and its movement. I used the different header tags to make word and phrases like "thrust" and "The stone flowed" larger and more noticeable. I also used the strong and emphasis tags to draw attention to words like "stillness," and "flowing," and "pause." The horizontal lines helped separate and create tension to the sentence about the "frozen explosion of granite," making it feel more dramatic.

I also moved the title and author to the bottom so it wouldn't be the first thing the reader saw. I wanted the formatting to make the stone feel alive and like the main subject and create a more ominous interpretation of the text.
